I USED TO THINK I KNEW WHAT LOVE WAS

For almost eight or nine years, I kept asking myself the same question.

What actually is love?

Not the Instagram version.

Not the movie version.

Not the "I love you" that people casually throw around.

What does the word actually mean?

Is it attraction?

Is it attachment?

Is it some chemical thing the brain does?

Is it simply two people liking each other so much that they don't want to be apart?

I asked different people.

I watched videos.

I read articles.

I listened to spiritual gurus.

Everybody had an answer.

And somehow none of the answers stayed constant.

So eventually I stopped searching.

Then you happened.

And suddenly the question became much harder because now it wasn't some philosophical question anymore.

It was sitting inside my chest.

Before you came into my life, there was this weird feeling of incompleteness in me.

I used to work.

A lot.

I have always been that guy who wants to build things.

Since childhood I had this urge to make cool stuff. Electronics, computers, machines, whatever. I wanted to create things that made people go "what the hell is that?" and "how did you make this?"

That feeling of building something real is still one of the things that makes me feel alive.

I also became obsessed with improving myself.

I have this strange habit where I look backward at myself.

I ask, would the Eshan from 8th standard be proud of the person I am now?

Would that kid, who had his own insecurities and was bullied for being weak, look at me and think, yeah, this is what I wanted to become?

When the answer is yes, I feel proud.

But then I ask another question.

Would the Eshan from last year be proud of me?

And sometimes the answer is no.

That annoys me.

So I work again.

I try to become stronger.

Smarter.

Healthier.

Better.

I used to do all of that almost blindly.

I knew I wanted something, but sometimes I didn't even know what the "something" was.

Then you came into my life and the reason behind all of it changed.

Not because suddenly my goals disappeared.

Actually the opposite happened.

I wanted to work harder.

I wanted to get stronger.

I wanted to become more financially stable.

I wanted to become better at what I do.

I wanted to become a better man.

Because now there was someone whose happiness mattered to me.

Someone I wanted to protect.

Someone whose life I wanted to make a little better.

And slowly I started realizing that maybe this strange feeling wasn't pulling me away from my ambitions.

Maybe it was giving them a direction.

I remember what my dad once told me.

I asked him what kind of girl would be best for me.

He said, "Your counter-opposite, but with a common connection."

I was like, what the hell does that even mean?

Then he explained it.

He said, imagine getting a girl who is exactly like you. Always inside computers, work, projects, goals, plans, always trying to build something.

Would you really want to spend an entire lifetime talking about work with someone who is doing exactly what you're doing?

Maybe.

But what are the odds that you would both fit perfectly?

Then he said, imagine someone completely opposite.

Someone who pulls you out of your work for a while.

Someone who shows you a world beyond the one you've built inside your head.

Sometimes she might get on your nerves.

Sometimes she might not understand why you think the way you do.

But she might also give you a kind of happiness that your work alone never could.

I wasn't convinced.

Not even slightly.

Until I got you.

And then I realized the biggest mistake I had been making.

I was trying to make you more like me.

I was carrying around all these beliefs about how a person should behave, how things should be done, how a relationship should work, and without realizing it I was trying to put my own perfectionism into you.

But you aren't me.

And thank god you're not.

That's one of the reasons I love you.

You're different.

You have your own way of thinking.

Your own habits.

Your own chaos.

Your own emotions.

Your own way of looking at the world.

And I slowly started realizing that uniqueness isn't a problem to solve.

It is the point.

I have spent so much of my life chasing perfection, even though I know perfection doesn't exist. It has caused me to overthink projects, leave things incomplete, and make everything harder than it needs to be.

I was doing the same thing in my relationship.

Trying to optimize a human being.

Which is probably one of the dumbest things I have ever done.

Because people aren't machines.

And love definitely isn't an engineering project.

The weirdest thing is that I didn't understand what love was until I started experiencing what happened to me during our fights.

Every time we fought, something happened inside me that I couldn't logically explain.

I would get anxious.

I would get scared.

I would lose focus.

I could be doing something important and my brain would still be somewhere else.

My heart would be screaming about one thing.

What if I lose her?

I have trained myself to be logical in so many parts of life.

I can look at a problem, break it down, study it, find the variables and work toward a solution.

But when it came to you, logic could not simply switch the feeling off.

It was like my heart had decided something without consulting my brain.

It wanted to stay.

It wanted to protect you.

It wanted you to be happy.

And whenever you were sad because of something I had done, it hurt in a completely different way.

That's when I started connecting all the dots.

Maybe this is love.

Not because I am attracted to someone.

Not because I enjoy talking to someone.

Not even simply because I don't want to lose someone.

Maybe love is when another person's wellbeing starts becoming connected to your own.

When their happiness can make your day.

When their sadness can sit inside your chest even when you're hundreds of kilometers away.

When you don't just want their attention.

You want their life to become better.

For me, love started to feel like protection.

Not control.

Protection.

Wanting to keep something beautiful safe.

There is that old line I keep thinking about: when you like a flower, you pluck it. When you love a flower, you protect it and water it every day.

I think I finally understand why that idea stayed with me.

I don't want to own you.

I don't want to turn you into someone I imagined in my head.

I want you to be yourself.

I want you to grow.

And I want to be there while you do.

That doesn't mean I will always be perfect.

I know I have messed up.

I know I have made you cry.

I know my moodiness, my frustration, my stubbornness and my need to fix everything have hurt you sometimes.

There were times I tried so hard to remove your problems that I became one of them.

That is something I genuinely regret.

I am not perfect.

I am trying to become better.

Not because I am scared that you will find someone better.

I want to become better because I love you and because I also want you to be proud of the man standing beside you.

So maybe after all these years, I finally have an answer.

Maybe love is not one definition.

Maybe it changes depending on who you are and who you meet.

But for me, it is this:

Love is when someone's existence gives your life a new purpose.

It is when you want to see them happy even when there is nothing in it for you.

It is when you want to protect their heart.

It is when you care about the boring parts, the irritating parts, the childish parts and the difficult parts.

It is when you don't just want someone for the perfect version of their life.

You want the real thing.

The messy version too.

And maybe most importantly...

love is when you look at a person and think,

I don't want to change who you are.

I just want to be there while you become whoever you are meant to become.

So yea.

After searching for an answer for years, I think I finally found one.

Maybe this weird feeling I have inside me really is love.

And maybe...

I am truly in love with you, Siddhi.
